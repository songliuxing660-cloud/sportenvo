Commercial Site Planning Guide

Padel Court Dimensions and Space Requirements

Quick Answer: The standard padel court playing area is 20 m long and 10 m wide. This is the playing footprint, not the total project site requirement. A commercial project needs additional space for player access, circulation, drainage, foundation structure, maintenance and, where applicable, spectator areas, roof support columns and multi-court layouts. Indoor projects must also consider clear height above the playing area. Final site planning should reflect the specific project conditions, court configuration and local engineering requirements.

What Are the Standard Padel Court Dimensions?

A standard padel court has a playing area of 20 m × 10 m. This measurement applies to single-court installations across all SPORTENVO commercial court systems, including Panoramic, Super Panoramic, Mobile / Modular and FORCE-HX courts.

DimensionValue
Court Length20 m
Court Width10 m
Playing Area20 m × 10 m

These are the playing dimensions common to the sport. The total project footprint, foundation area and structural envelope will be larger once all project planning requirements are considered.

Padel Court Layout: Glass, Mesh and Playing Area

A padel court enclosure combines tempered glass panels with welded mesh sections supported by the structural steel frame. The glass is primarily used on the side and back walls where transparent viewing is important. Mesh sections are typically used in zones where full glass panels are not required or structurally appropriate.

SPORTENVO panoramic courts use 12 mm tempered glass as the main viewing wall element. The exact layout of glass, mesh and court entrances depends on the specific court model and project configuration. Review the individual commercial padel court systems for model-specific structural information.

How Much Total Space Is Needed for One Padel Court?

The 20 × 10 m playing area is only the starting point. A commercially planned padel court requires additional site area for the following:

  • Player access and circulation — entry doors, paths to and from the court
  • Perimeter maintenance access — space for cleaning, maintenance and inspection
  • Foundation structure — foundation footprint may extend beyond the playing area
  • Drainage — surface drainage direction and outlet locations
  • Structural supports — roof columns or lighting poles where applicable
  • Spectator space — bench or standing areas for commercial venues
  • Emergency access — required by local building regulations

The specific clearances required depend on the project design, court model, foundation type, roof requirements and local regulations. A universal total site figure cannot replace a project-specific layout review.

Indoor Padel Court Height Requirements

Indoor padel installations require adequate clear height above the playing area. The required height depends on the intended level of play, project standards, venue conditions and local building requirements.

Indoor projects should assess clear height early in the site review process, before confirming the court model or structural configuration. Ceiling structure, roof beams, services and lighting all need to be coordinated with the required playing clearance.

Indoor clear-height requirements should be confirmed according to project standards, venue conditions and the intended level of play. Review height and structural requirements with the venue engineer before finalizing the court layout.

Outdoor Padel Court Space Planning

Outdoor commercial padel courts require additional site planning considerations beyond playing dimensions:

  • Drainage — surface water direction and outlet design
  • Wind exposure — orientation and any adjacent structures affecting wind load on the court and roof
  • Foundation type — traditional concrete or modular steel foundation according to ground conditions
  • Court orientation — sun direction can affect playing conditions and spectator comfort
  • Lighting pole positions — placement must not conflict with court structure or access
  • Surrounding structures — fencing, walls or other structures adjacent to the court affect wind and access
  • Roof or canopy — if weather protection is required, the roof structure and drainage must be planned as part of the court layout

For outdoor projects in coastal or high-wind environments, the FORCE-HX high-wind padel court and the high-wind padel court planning guide provide additional structural guidance. For weather-protected projects, see the padel court roof system.

Space Requirements for Multiple Padel Courts

Commercial padel clubs commonly install two, four or more courts. Multi-court layouts require planning for the following in addition to individual court dimensions:

  • Court-to-court spacing — circulation aisles between courts for player movement
  • Spectator areas — viewing zones between or around courts
  • Reception and lounge — club operational area adjacent to the court zone
  • Lighting coverage — lighting design for the full court area and common zones
  • Service access — maintenance equipment access to each court
  • Emergency access — required corridors and exit routes for the full venue
  • Equipment and storage — ball machines, cleaning equipment, spare turf

Spacing between courts depends on project design, club concept, circulation comfort and local requirements. Court-to-court spacing should be determined through layout planning rather than assumed from a single standard dimension. See commercial padel court projects for reference layouts.

Foundation and Ground Planning

Foundation choice affects both the project area and the available design flexibility. Ground condition, level, drainage and bearing capacity should be assessed before confirming the foundation type.

FactorPlanning Consideration
Ground conditionStability, bearing capacity, settlement risk
Level and drainageSurface water direction, leveling requirements
Foundation typeTraditional concrete or modular steel
LevelingModular foundation adjustable up to 180 mm
AnchoringAnchoring requirements based on structural loads and wind exposure

The modular padel court foundation system provides up to 180 mm of height adjustment, which can help level the court on sites with minor ground variation. Review the modular foundation planning guide to assess whether this approach suits the project site.

Roof and Covered Court Planning

If a roof is required, the site must accommodate more than just the 20 × 10 m court footprint. A covered padel court project requires planning for:

  • Roof span — roof must cover the full court width and length, plus clearance
  • Support columns — column positions and foundation loads extend beyond the court area
  • Drainage — roof water must be directed to appropriate outlets
  • Wind load — roof increases wind resistance requirements and foundation loads
  • Maintenance — access to the roof structure and membrane

Review the padel court roof system and the padel court roof planning guide for roof size options and structural scope before including a roof in the site layout.

Panoramic vs Super Panoramic Layout Considerations

Both Panoramic and Super Panoramic courts share the same standard 20 × 10 m playing footprint. The difference between them lies in the structural configuration and degree of visual openness around the playing enclosure — not in the playing dimensions.

Super Panoramic courts place greater emphasis on reduced structural obstruction, which can affect column placement and glass panel layout. The playing area remains identical. Review the panoramic vs super panoramic comparison guide to understand the structural and visual differences relevant to venue layout decisions.

What Information Is Needed for a Padel Court Layout Plan?

Sharing specific project information early allows a layout review to be prepared before final court selection. Prepare the following:

  1. Project country and city — affects climate, local codes and structural requirements
  2. Indoor or outdoor application — determines height, drainage and weather considerations
  3. Number of courts — defines the total site area and layout complexity
  4. Available site dimensions — total usable area including any constraints
  5. Existing ground condition — soil type, drainage, existing structures
  6. Preferred court model — Panoramic, Super Panoramic, Mobile, FORCE-HX or other
  7. Foundation type — traditional concrete or modular steel preferred
  8. Roof requirement — fixed, retractable or none
  9. Spectator and circulation requirements — club seating, walkways, event space
  10. Target project schedule — production and installation timeline

Padel Court Dimensions FAQ

What are the standard padel court dimensions?

A standard padel court has a playing area of 20 m in length and 10 m in width. This is the playing footprint — the total site area required for a commercial project will be larger once access, circulation, foundation, drainage and structural support requirements are planned.

Is 20 × 10 m the total space required for a padel court?

No. The 20 × 10 m measurement is the playing area. A commercial project requires additional space for player access, circulation around the court, drainage, foundation support structure, maintenance access and, where applicable, spectator areas or roof support columns. The total project footprint should be determined based on the specific site and project layout.

How much extra space should be planned around the court?

Additional space requirements depend on the project configuration, site conditions and operational needs of the venue. Commercial projects typically need perimeter access for maintenance, player circulation, emergency access, foundation structure and any spectator or service areas. The exact clearances required should be determined based on the project layout and local requirements rather than a single universal figure.

What height is required for an indoor padel court?

Indoor clear-height requirements should be confirmed according to project standards, the intended level of play, venue conditions and local building requirements. Adequate clear height above the playing area is required to accommodate ball play. Indoor projects should review height requirements early in the site assessment process.

Can multiple padel courts be installed side by side?

Yes. Commercial padel clubs commonly install two or more courts. Multi-court layouts require planning for court-to-court spacing, circulation aisles between courts, spectator areas, lighting coverage, service access, emergency access and overall site drainage. Spacing between courts depends on project design, circulation needs and local requirements.

Does a padel court roof require extra space?

Yes. A roof system requires space for structural support columns, roof span, drainage and maintenance access beyond the 20 × 10 m court footprint. A retractable roof system may have additional mechanical clearance requirements. The roof structure and foundation loads must be reviewed as part of the overall project layout.

Does the foundation affect the required site area?

Foundation type and design can influence the layout and clearance requirements around the court. A modular steel foundation provides up to 180 mm of adjustable height, which can assist leveling on uneven sites. Foundation column positions, edge distances and anchoring requirements should be reviewed as part of the site layout plan.

What site information is needed before layout planning?

Before confirming a padel court layout, it helps to have: project country and city, indoor or outdoor application, number of courts, available site dimensions, existing ground condition, preferred court model, foundation type, roof requirement, spectator or circulation requirements, and target project schedule.
