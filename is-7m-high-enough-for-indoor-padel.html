Indoor Padel Buyer Guide · 2026

Is 7m High Enough for an Indoor Padel Court?

Quick Answer: FIP rules state a minimum free height of 6 m throughout the court and suggest 8 m minimum free height for new facilities. A 7 m venue sits between those two numbers. It can be playable when genuinely unobstructed, but a commercial club should also evaluate beams, lights, HVAC, player expectations and competing venues before committing to the building.

Why Are Club Owners Asking About 7 m?

Recent padel community discussions repeatedly show the same real-estate problem: an otherwise attractive hall has a lowest point around 6.7–7 m. Players often describe that height as playable but less desirable when a higher-clearance alternative exists. That is useful market feedback, but it is not a technical standard.

Community signal is not regulation.

Use player discussions to understand expectations. Use FIP rules, measured building geometry and local engineering to decide whether the venue works.

FIP Minimum vs New-Facility Recommendation

Clear heightHow to read it
6 mFIP minimum free height throughout the court, with no obstructing elements in the volume.
7 mAbove the stated minimum, but not the same as a new facility designed around the 8 m recommendation.
8 mFIP suggestion for minimum free height in new facilities.
8 m+Provides additional margin for high lobs and for keeping lights and building services outside the playing volume.

What Actually Makes a 7 m Hall Feel Low?

Roof trusses

A nominally tall building can lose useful clearance at trusses. Measure the lowest obstruction over every proposed court position.

Lighting

Lights must not intrude into the required clear volume. Low fixtures can also create glare during overhead shots.

HVAC, sprinklers and cable trays

Warehouse conversions often lose more usable height to services than buyers expect from the real-estate brochure.

Player expectations

Frequent and experienced players use higher lobs more aggressively. A venue that targets repeat players should evaluate experience as well as compliance.

How to Measure an Indoor Venue Before Signing

  1. Measure the lowest unobstructed point over every proposed court.
  2. Plot trusses, beams, lights, HVAC, sprinklers and cable trays on the same drawing.
  3. Overlay the 20 × 10 m court positions plus access and circulation.
  4. Check gabled roofs at both ends of the court, not only at the center ridge.
  5. Confirm local fire, building and accessibility requirements before the lease or equipment order.

For complete venue planning, use How to Build a Padel Club together with the dimensions guide.

When Can a 7 m Venue Still Make Sense?

A 7 m hall may still deserve consideration when clearance is truly unobstructed, the location and lease are strong, alternative facilities are limited and the club is aimed primarily at recreational or social play. But “playable” and “commercially competitive” are different questions.

If another viable venue provides 8 m or more of clear height at a manageable cost, that extra margin can improve both court planning and the player experience.

Indoor Padel Hall Checklist

  • Lowest unobstructed height above each court
  • Truss and beam geometry
  • Lighting height and glare risk
  • HVAC, sprinklers and mechanical services
  • Number of courts and circulation
  • Floor and foundation condition
  • Fire access and local requirements
  • Target player profile and nearby competition
  • Lease term versus permanent building limitations

Indoor Padel Height FAQ

Is 7 meters high enough for an indoor padel court?

Seven meters can be playable if it is genuinely unobstructed across the court, but it sits between the FIP minimum of 6 meters and the 8-meter minimum free height suggested for new facilities. A commercial decision should also consider beams, lights, HVAC, player level and competing venues.

What is the minimum height for a padel court?

The FIP Rules of Padel state a minimum free height of 6 meters throughout the court with no obstructing elements in that volume. For new facilities, FIP suggests a minimum free height of 8 meters throughout the court.

Do lights and roof beams count when measuring padel court height?

Yes. Clear height is the unobstructed vertical space available for play. Beams, lighting, ducts, sprinklers and other services can reduce usable clearance even when the nominal building height is higher.

Should a club sign a lease before confirming clear height?

No. Measure the lowest relevant obstruction over the full proposed court positions, map beams and services, confirm the layout and review local requirements before committing to the venue.
