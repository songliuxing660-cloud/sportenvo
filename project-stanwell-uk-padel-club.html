Padel Boutique project in Stanwell, UK

A commercial padel venue currently operating three courts — one outdoor and two covered. The company project archive also records a broader phased development scope including additional courts, roof infrastructure and support facilities.

Project Snapshot

A commercial venue shaped by its operating model.

This case study focuses on the project facts that matter to buyers: court mix, venue conditions, recorded supply scope and the planning decisions behind the finished facility.

Project Challenges

What had to be solved before delivery.

01

All-weather operation

The UK climate made weather protection important for maintaining usable court hours without enclosing the entire venue.

02

Multi-revenue club layout

The facility needed to support recreational play, coaching, memberships, corporate bookings and on-site customer service.

03

Integrated supporting facilities

Reception, player amenities, circulation and court access had to work as one commercial venue rather than separate products.

Recorded Supply Scope

What the project package included.

01

Panoramic court package

The company archive records a broader six-court development package; current independent venue listings show three courts in operation. SPORTENVO presents the current operating count separately from the archived development scope.

02

Half-enclosure roof

A clear-span aluminium roof structure covered selected courts while preserving natural ventilation and court visibility.

03

Container reception

A customised 20 ft container unit provided reception, pro-shop and coaching administration functions.

04

Export & installation support

The project record includes export packing, international shipping support, technical drawings and installation guidance.

Buyer Takeaways

Use this project as a planning reference.

Start with the operating model.

Court quantity, court mix and covered / uncovered areas should reflect bookings, coaching, events and the target customer experience.

Define the site interfaces.

Confirm roof clearance, ground or slab condition, access, drainage, utilities and local installation responsibilities before manufacturing.

Compare the complete scope.

Evaluate courts, technical documents, packing, logistics and installation support together rather than comparing a single headline equipment price.
