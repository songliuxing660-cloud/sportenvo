Padel Surface Buyer Guide · 2026

Padel Court Turf: 12mm, Sand, Dtex and Maintenance

Quick answer: turf quality cannot be judged by pile height alone. A commercial padel surface should be evaluated as a system: fiber geometry, pile height, yarn mass, backing, infill, drainage, installation quality and maintenance all affect ball response, traction and service life.

Why Padel Players Notice the Turf Immediately

Recent owner and player discussions repeatedly focus on the same symptoms: one court feels faster, another feels dead, sand collects at the edges, traction changes, or the ball behaves differently after rain. Those complaints are useful because they show that the surface is not a cosmetic finish. It is part of the playing system.

For a commercial buyer, the correct question is not simply “Is it 12 mm?” It is “What complete turf specification am I buying, how is it installed, and how will it be maintained at this venue?”

Do not compare padel turf by pile height alone.

Ask for the full datasheet, infill requirement, backing and drainage information, installation method and maintenance instructions.

SPORTENVO's current commercial padel systems use 12 mm professional padel turf in selected configurations. That does not mean every 12 mm product is equivalent. Two products with the same pile height can use very different fiber shapes, yarn weights, tuft densities and backings.

What Does Dtex Mean?

Dtex is a yarn mass measurement. It is useful because it helps describe the amount of polymer in the fiber bundle, but it should not be used as a stand-alone quality score. A higher number does not automatically guarantee better play, and a lower number does not automatically mean the turf is unsuitable.

  • Ask for Dtex together with yarn type and profile.
  • Check stitch rate, gauge and density.
  • Confirm primary and secondary backing construction.
  • Confirm tuft-lock or withdrawal performance where test data is available.
  • Compare the same test method across suppliers.

Why Is There Sand on a Padel Court?

Silica-sand infill supports the turf fibers, helps stabilize the playing surface and contributes to the intended playing characteristics. The exact quantity depends on the turf design. Modern textured systems may hide more of the sand below the fiber tips, which can make two correctly maintained surfaces look very different.

Problems appear when sand migrates and is not redistributed. Heavy accumulation around the sides and back can change traction and ball response, while depleted high-traffic areas may wear differently.

What buyers should specify

  1. Approved infill material and grading.
  2. Target infill quantity for the selected turf.
  3. Initial installation and brushing procedure.
  4. Maintenance frequency for the expected booking volume.
  5. How the supplier recommends topping up or redistributing sand.

Drainage Matters as Much as Turf

A permeable turf cannot compensate for a poor base. Outdoor court performance depends on the complete drainage path: turf permeability, sub-base or slab design, falls, perimeter channels and discharge points. If water has nowhere to go, replacing the turf alone will not solve a wet-court problem.

Use the padel court drainage guide before finalizing an outdoor surface or foundation.

Installation Quality Changes the Result

Even a good turf can perform badly when seams, adhesive, stretching, infill or line installation are inconsistent. Before handover, inspect the complete surface rather than only the material certificate.

SeamsFlat, closed and aligned with no lifting edges

InfillEvenly distributed with no obvious piles or bare high-traffic zones

Commercial Maintenance Plan

The correct schedule depends on player volume, climate and turf system, but a commercial club should plan recurring inspection rather than waiting until the court feels wrong.

  • Remove leaves, dust and foreign material.
  • Redistribute sand away from accumulation zones.
  • Brush high-traffic areas as recommended by the turf supplier.
  • Inspect seams, lines and perimeter edges.
  • Check drainage after heavy rain.
  • Record wear so replacement can be planned rather than reactive.

Questions to Put in an RFQ

  1. What is the exact pile height, yarn type, Dtex, gauge and stitch rate?
  2. What is the total turf density or face weight?
  3. What backing system is used?
  4. How much and what type of sand infill is required?
  5. What permeability or drainage data is available?
  6. What installation materials and method are specified?
  7. What maintenance routine is required for a commercial club?
  8. What is the turf warranty and what maintenance records are required to keep it valid?

Padel Court Turf FAQ

What pile height is used for padel court turf?

Commercial padel turf is commonly supplied around the 10–12 mm range, but the correct selection depends on yarn geometry, density, backing, sand infill, drainage and intended level of play.

Is 12 mm turf good for a padel court?

Yes, 12 mm is a common commercial configuration and is used in SPORTENVO systems. The full material and installation specification still needs to be checked.

Why is sand used in padel turf?

Approved silica-sand infill helps stabilize fibers and contributes to the intended playing characteristics. The quantity should follow the turf manufacturer's specification.

How often should padel turf be brushed?

There is no single interval for every venue. Inspect frequently and follow the selected turf system's maintenance plan based on use and climate.
