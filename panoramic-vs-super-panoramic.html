Court Comparison Guide

Panoramic vs Super Panoramic Padel Court: Which Should You Choose?

Quick Answer: Both Panoramic and Super Panoramic courts use the standard 20 m × 10 m padel playing footprint. The main differences relate to structural layout, visual openness, sightlines and commercial positioning. Panoramic courts suit a wide range of commercial projects. Super Panoramic is often selected when visual openness and premium venue presentation are higher priorities. Neither option is universally better. The right choice depends on venue type, project positioning, budget, engineering requirements and operating goals.

What Is a Panoramic Padel Court?

A panoramic padel court is a professional commercial padel court designed with large tempered glass viewing areas around the playing enclosure. It provides strong spectator visibility while using a structural frame that balances visual openness with commercial practicality and structural reliability.

Panoramic courts are widely used for commercial padel clubs, sports centers, hotels, resorts and outdoor venues. The system combines 12 mm tempered glass, galvanized steel structure, professional turf and LED lighting in a package suited to a broad range of international commercial projects.

Panoramic is often the right choice when:

  • A reliable, commercially proven court system is the priority
  • Strong spectator visibility is required across a range of venue types
  • Project efficiency, commercial practicality and cost management are key criteria
  • Outdoor club or resort projects require a well-balanced all-round system

What Is a Super Panoramic Padel Court?

A super panoramic padel court is designed with a greater focus on visual openness and reduced structural obstruction around the playing enclosure. The configuration creates cleaner sightlines for spectators and a more open visual presentation — qualities that matter for premium club environments and showcase venues.

Super Panoramic courts are often selected for premium padel clubs, indoor facilities, hotel showpiece courts and venues where court aesthetics are part of the member or visitor experience.

Super Panoramic may be preferred when:

  • Premium visual presentation and open sightlines are the primary design criteria
  • The venue is positioned at the high end of the market
  • Spectator experience, photography or event presentation are important considerations
  • Indoor architectural integration requires a cleaner visual result

Which Offers Better Spectator Visibility?

Super Panoramic courts are designed around reduced structural obstruction, which provides a more open visual experience for spectators. The layout creates cleaner sightlines around the full playing enclosure.

Panoramic courts already provide strong spectator visibility through large tempered glass panels. For many commercial venues — including busy outdoor clubs and sports centers — the panoramic system delivers excellent viewing conditions that fully meet the operational requirements.

The decision about which offers "better" visibility depends on the specific venue concept and how much premium is placed on visual openness versus other project priorities. Neither approach delivers zero obstruction.

Which Is Better for Commercial Padel Clubs?

There is no universal answer. Both are used by successful commercial padel clubs. The decision should follow specific venue priorities:

  • Club positioning — a premium club targeting high-end members may favour Super Panoramic for visual brand impact; a sports-focused club may prefer Panoramic for performance and practicality
  • Target customer — member expectations and price point affect the appropriate court presentation level
  • Venue design — architecture, interior concept and court layout influence which system integrates better
  • Available budget — project scope should drive the decision, not aspirational specification alone
  • Number of courts — multi-court clubs may select different models for different zones of the facility
  • Event requirements — clubs hosting events or livestreaming may value Super Panoramic's cleaner viewing angles
  • Visual branding — court appearance as part of the brand identity may favour one approach over the other
  • Local engineering conditions — structural requirements, wind exposure and foundation conditions affect project scope

View commercial padel court projects for reference examples of both systems in operation.

Indoor vs Outdoor Considerations

Both systems can be installed in indoor and outdoor environments. Project environment may affect which system makes more sense:

  • Indoor projects — clear height, ceiling structure, lighting integration and floor/foundation conditions affect layout; Super Panoramic's cleaner appearance often suits premium indoor venues
  • Outdoor projects — drainage, foundation, wind exposure and corrosion protection requirements should be reviewed; both systems can be configured for outdoor use
  • Wind and coastal environments — for projects in exposed or coastal locations, structural wind performance is the priority; the FORCE-HX high-wind court may be more appropriate than either panoramic variant
  • Roof requirements — where a covered court is required, the padel court roof system should be assessed alongside the court selection

Does Super Panoramic Cost More?

Project price depends on the complete configuration — court model, foundation, glass, turf, LED lighting, roof, shipping, installation and customization. It is not accurate to state that one system is always more expensive without reviewing the full project scope.

Buyers should compare like-for-like quotations based on the same project specification. Factors that affect total project cost include:

  • Court model specification
  • Foundation type and ground preparation
  • Shipping destination and logistics
  • Installation scope
  • Roof or canopy requirements
  • Color, customization and accessories

Review the padel court cost guide for a full breakdown of commercial project cost factors.

Court Dimensions and Site Planning

Both Panoramic and Super Panoramic courts use the standard padel playing area of 20 m × 10 m. Playing dimensions are the same across both systems.

Total site planning depends on more than the playing footprint. Additional space is required for access, circulation, structural support, foundation, drainage, spectator zones and, where applicable, roof support columns. Review the padel court dimensions and site planning guide when assessing site suitability for either system.

Foundation Requirements

Foundation selection is the same category of decision for both systems — and depends on ground conditions, court type, wind exposure, anchoring and project type rather than on which panoramic variant is selected.

Key factors:

  • Ground type and bearing capacity
  • Drainage and site leveling requirements
  • Anchoring design for the local wind environment
  • Permanent or flexible installation intent
  • Roof load if a covered court is planned

Review the padel court foundation guide and the modular foundation system to evaluate foundation options for either court type.

Panoramic vs Super Panoramic FAQ

What is the main difference between Panoramic and Super Panoramic Padel Courts?

Both use tempered glass to provide spectator visibility around the playing enclosure. The main difference is the degree of visual openness: the Super Panoramic configuration places greater emphasis on reducing structural obstruction and creating a more open visual presentation around the court. Panoramic courts are reliable commercial systems for a wide range of venues; Super Panoramic is often selected when premium visual presentation and maximum sightline openness are higher priorities.

Which court offers more open sightlines?

Super Panoramic courts are designed with a greater focus on reducing structural obstruction around the viewing areas, which can provide more visually open sightlines for spectators. Panoramic courts already provide strong spectator visibility for many commercial venues. The difference depends on the structural configuration and layout of each system.

Is Super Panoramic always better than Panoramic?

No. Neither option is universally better. Both are professional commercial padel court systems. Panoramic courts are widely used for commercial clubs, sports centers and outdoor venues where a reliable complete system is the priority. Super Panoramic is often selected for premium clubs, indoor facilities and showcase venues where visual openness and venue presentation are the primary design criteria.

Which option is more suitable for a commercial padel club?

The most suitable option depends on club positioning, target customer, venue design, budget, number of courts and event requirements. A panoramic court suits most commercial club projects with a strong performance-to-cost balance. A super panoramic court may be preferred for premium positioning, showcase events or where visual openness is a key selling point. Neither is universally more suitable.

Are the court dimensions different?

No. Both Panoramic and Super Panoramic courts use the standard padel playing area of 20 m × 10 m. The difference between them lies in structural configuration and visual design, not in playing dimensions.

Can both court types be installed outdoors?

Yes. Both Panoramic and Super Panoramic courts can be installed in outdoor commercial environments. Structural configuration, surface treatment, foundation, anchoring and local wind requirements should be reviewed for each outdoor project. For coastal or high-wind environments, the FORCE-HX reinforced court may be more appropriate.

Does Super Panoramic cost more?

Project price depends on the complete configuration — court model, foundation, glass, turf, lighting, shipping, installation and any roof system. It is not possible to state that one system is always more expensive without evaluating the full project scope. Buyers should compare like-for-like quotations based on the same project specification.

What project information is needed before choosing a court?

Useful information includes: project country and city, number of courts, indoor or outdoor application, available site dimensions, existing ground condition, preferred court model or concept, foundation type, roof requirement, venue positioning and commercial goals, and target project schedule.
