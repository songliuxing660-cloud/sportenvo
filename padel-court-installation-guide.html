Installation & Delivery Guide

Padel Court Installation Guide: From Site Readiness to Handover

Quick Answer: Padel court installation starts before the container arrives. The site, foundation or approved base, drainage, access, unloading plan and local responsibilities should be confirmed against the project drawings first. Court assembly then progresses through structure, mesh, glass, turf, lighting and final checks. The exact sequence and responsibilities remain project-specific.

Installation Starts Before Shipment

A commercial padel court should not be shipped into an unresolved site. Before production is released or shipment is booked, confirm the site interfaces that determine whether installation can proceed without rework.

  • Site dimensions — playing area, circulation, entrances, clearances and any roof or building interfaces.
  • Foundation / base — approved dimensions, levels, drainage and anchor strategy.
  • Access — truck or container unloading, storage and lifting route.
  • Local works — civil, electrical, drainage, lifting equipment and permits.
  • Responsibility split — what SPORTENVO supplies, what the local team installs and what local contractors complete.

Use the Padel Court Dimensions & Site Planning Guide and Foundation Guide before installation planning is finalized.

Site Readiness Checklist Before the Court Arrives

Before mobilizing an installation team, confirm:

  • the latest approved court and foundation drawings are being used;
  • the installation area is clear and accessible;
  • finished foundation / base dimensions match the project drawings;
  • levels and drainage paths have been checked;
  • anchor locations or modular-base positions are defined;
  • there is a safe unloading and temporary storage area;
  • glass lifting / handling equipment and labor are available if locally required;
  • electrical interfaces are prepared for lighting;
  • roof, building or adjacent works will not conflict with the court installation sequence.

Foundation and Anchoring Must Match the Installed Court

The court structure, anchors and supporting foundation form one load path. A correct court package cannot compensate for an unsuitable base or incorrect anchor interface.

For fixed projects, the civil foundation or structural slab must be checked against the selected court. For flexible sites, a Modular Foundation Base may be reviewed. For coastal and high-wind sites, the foundation and anchors become even more critical; see the High-Wind Padel Court Guide.

Unloading, Storage and Component Protection

Installation quality can be damaged before assembly if components are unloaded or stored poorly. The local delivery plan should consider:

  • safe access for the transport vehicle;
  • lifting capacity for heavy or fragile components;
  • weather-protected temporary storage where required;
  • glass rack handling and edge protection;
  • clear component identification so installation follows the correct sequence;
  • protection of coated or galvanized steel from impact damage during handling.

Steel Structure Assembly and Alignment

The structural frame establishes the geometry for mesh, glass, doors and lighting. Installation teams should follow the latest approved assembly information rather than relying on generic court dimensions alone.

  • Set out the court from verified control points.
  • Assemble the primary structure in the documented sequence.
  • Check alignment and verticality before final tightening.
  • Confirm structural connections and visible hardware before glass installation.
  • Keep the installation area controlled so completed coated surfaces are not unnecessarily damaged.

Glass Installation Requires Controlled Handling

Tempered glass is a critical part of the padel enclosure and requires appropriate lifting, support and edge protection. The correct glass position, fasteners, protective interfaces and final clearances should follow the confirmed project documentation.

SPORTENVO court configurations commonly use 12 mm tempered glass where specified. The actual glass and hardware scope should be checked against the quotation and drawings for the project being installed.

Turf and Playing-Surface Completion

Turf installation should begin only when the supporting surface is ready, clean and free of work that could contaminate or damage the finished playing surface. The final turf finish depends on the specified system, joint layout and project conditions.

Drainage performance is also affected by the base and surrounding civil works. If water management is unresolved, review the Padel Court Drainage Guide before final handover.

Lighting and Local Electrical Coordination

Lighting installation is not only about mounting fixtures. The project should coordinate fixture position, aiming, electrical supply, cabling, local electrical protection and any building or roof conflicts.

Use the Padel Court Lighting Guide for planning context. Final electrical work should comply with the applicable project requirements and agreed local responsibilities.

How Roof Projects Change Installation

A Padel Court with Roof changes the installation sequence because roof structure, court structure, foundations, drainage and lifting access interact. The roof should not be treated as an accessory added after the court is already fixed.

Before installation begins, confirm:

  • roof column and court positions;
  • clear height and structural clearances;
  • foundation reactions and anchor zones;
  • drainage and discharge paths;
  • lifting sequence and temporary access;
  • local structural / permit responsibilities.

Final QC and Handover

Before the venue is handed over, the project team should review the installed condition and document any remaining local work. A handover check can include:

  • visible structural alignment and connection condition;
  • glass, mesh and door condition;
  • turf finish and playing-area cleanliness;
  • lighting operation where electrical scope is complete;
  • visible safety items and protective details;
  • completed documentation and maintenance notes;
  • agreed after-sales contact route.

Padel Court Installation FAQ

What should be ready before delivery?

Confirm dimensions, foundation / base, level, drainage, access, lifting, storage and the local responsibility split before the court arrives.

Who installs the court?

It depends on the commercial scope. A local team may install with supplier drawings and guidance, or additional on-site support can be agreed separately.

Must the foundation be complete first?

Yes. The approved support condition should be complete and checked before structural assembly begins.

What is the normal installation sequence?

Site check, unloading, base / anchors, structure, mesh and glass, turf, lighting, final QC and handover. The exact sequence is project-specific.

When is glass installed?

After the supporting structure and interfaces are ready, using the handling method and hardware defined for the project.

What causes delays?

Unfinished foundations, incorrect levels, drainage problems, missing lifting access, late roof changes and unclear local responsibilities are common risks.

Does a roof change installation?

Yes. Roof reactions, columns, drainage, clear height and lifting sequence should be coordinated with the court and foundation before installation.

What should handover include?

Document the installed configuration, visible QC, supplied documentation, outstanding local work, maintenance notes and after-sales contacts.

Installation note

Installation duration and labor requirements vary by court model, quantity, site readiness, local team and project conditions. SPORTENVO should confirm those items against the actual project rather than publishing a universal installation time.
