Padel Conversion Buyer Guide · 2026

Can You Convert a Tennis Court Into Padel Courts?

Quick Answer: Potentially, yes — but the existing tennis surface is only one part of the decision. A padel conversion introduces a steel enclosure, tempered glass, anchors, turf, lighting and different drainage details. Verify the full footprint, what sits below the surface, structural suitability, drainage, access and local approvals before ordering courts.

Why Tennis-to-Padel Conversion Is a Real Buyer Question

Recent club-owner discussions show investors looking at old tennis courts, community centers and underused club space as possible padel sites. The repeated questions are practical: can the old base be reused, can two courts fit, what changes in drainage, and will planning or neighbor noise become a problem?

Conversion is a site project, not a product swap.

The safest route is to verify what can genuinely be reused before defining the padel equipment and civil-work scope.

Step 1: Measure the Full Site, Not Just the Tennis Lines

A regulation padel playing court measures 20 × 10 m internally, but the complete installation needs additional room for structure, entrances, circulation, maintenance, drainage and local requirements. Existing tennis fences, lights, retaining walls and neighboring uses can reduce the area that is actually available.

Start with the Padel Court Dimensions & Site Planning Guide before deciding whether the site supports one, two or more courts.

Step 2: Find Out What Is Under the Tennis Surface

Existing concrete

Do not judge it only by appearance. Thickness, reinforcement, cracking, levels, drainage and anchor capacity need review for the new structural system.

Asphalt or porous construction

A surface that drains well for tennis still needs a separate structural assessment before it is used with padel anchors, glass and enclosure loads.

Unknown sub-base

If construction records are missing, investigation may be needed before a supplier or local engineer can define the foundation approach.

Level differences

Settlement or slope can affect glass alignment, doors, turf and drainage once the new court is installed.

Compare options in the Concrete vs Modular Padel Foundation Guide.

Step 3: Check Drainage Before Adding Glass and Turf

Existing tennis drainage may not automatically suit the new court layout. Padel enclosures, perimeter details, turf and future roof options can change how water moves through and around the site. Confirm existing falls, channels, outlets and discharge points before locking the court positions.

If a canopy or roof may be added later, coordinate roof drainage and foundation reactions early. See the Padel Court Roof Construction & Planning Guide.

Step 4: Planning Permission, Noise and Lighting Can Decide the Project

Local approvals vary, so there is no universal permit answer. A conversion can raise issues that were less significant for the original tennis use: enclosure height, new lighting, later operating hours, player noise, racket and glass impacts, drainage, parking and proximity to homes.

Recent UK-focused padel discussions repeatedly flag planning and neighbor noise as issues to investigate before construction. Treat that as buyer intelligence, then confirm the actual requirements with the local planning authority and project consultants.

Do this before ordering equipment.

Confirm planning status, operating hours, lighting restrictions and any acoustic requirements before finalizing the court layout or delivery schedule.

Tennis Court to Padel Conversion FAQ

Can you convert a tennis court into padel courts?

Potentially, yes, but it is not a simple surface replacement. The site must be checked for usable dimensions, structural base, anchoring, drainage, access, lighting, local planning requirements and the intended number of padel courts.

Can two padel courts fit on one tennis court?

Some sites may physically accommodate two padel courts within the wider area used by a tennis court and its run-off space, but there is no universal answer. Measure the complete footprint and include structures, entrances, circulation, maintenance, drainage and local requirements before fixing court quantity.

Can an existing tennis court slab be reused for padel?

Only after it is inspected and verified for the new structural and drainage requirements. Padel adds steel, glass, anchors and different load paths. Surface appearance alone does not confirm that the existing base is suitable.

Does tennis-to-padel conversion need planning permission?

Approval requirements vary by jurisdiction and site. Change of use, lighting, enclosure height, operating hours, noise, drainage and proximity to neighbors can affect the local approval process. Confirm requirements with the relevant local authority before construction.

Is a modular foundation useful for a tennis-court conversion?

It can be considered for selected sites where reducing permanent civil work or preserving future flexibility matters, but it still requires suitable ground, drainage, leveling and project-specific structural review.
