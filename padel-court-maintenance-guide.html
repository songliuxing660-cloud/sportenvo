Padel Court Maintenance: Schedule, Costs and Replacement Planning

Quick answer: a padel court does not have one maintenance task or one universal annual cost. The operating plan should cover the playing surface, glass, steel enclosure, drainage, lighting, net and—where fitted—the roof or canopy, while the budget should separate routine upkeep from future component replacement.

Why Maintenance Is Becoming a Bigger Buyer Question

Padel club owners increasingly ask about the cost of cleaning, resurfacing, glass care and turf upkeep once a venue is operating. Reddit discussions include club operators comparing maintenance experience and outdoor-court owners asking how often textured turf needs sand redistribution or top-up. These comments are anecdotal, but they point to a real planning gap: many first-time buyers budget the court purchase and installation but not the maintenance system that begins on opening day.

Maintenance should be designed before the court opens.

Request an operation and maintenance manual at handover, assign responsibility for every component and record inspections. This is safer than waiting for the court to look worn.

A Practical Maintenance Schedule

The 2025 SAPCA Code of Practice for padel courts recommends regular cleaning and notes that a busy venue may need basic cleaning daily rather than weekly. The correct schedule still needs to be adapted to playing hours, weather, surrounding vegetation and the selected turf and court system.

FrequencyTypical commercial checks
Daily / opening checksRemove obvious litter and hazards; look for broken glass, loose hardware, water, damaged doors, net problems and unsafe playing-surface conditions.
WeeklyClean the turf and perimeter areas, inspect drainage, check sand distribution, glass, doors, mesh, net and visible lighting condition.
MonthlyReview high-traffic turf zones, seams and lines; inspect fasteners and coating damage; clean glass more thoroughly; test lighting and controls; inspect gutters and downpipes.
Quarterly / seasonalReview infill quantity and redistribution, drainage performance after rain, corrosion exposure, anchors and structural connections, and any weather-related movement or damage.
6–12 monthsSchedule specialist turf maintenance when required, including deeper cleaning, decompaction, sand correction and seam repairs; complete a documented court-condition review.
Before / after severe weatherCheck exposed structures, glass, roof elements, drainage, loose objects, fasteners and the site condition according to the project's wind and storm procedures.

This is a planning framework, not a substitute for the supplier manuals. Temporary, mobile or base-weighted court systems can have different inspection requirements.

Padel Turf Maintenance

The playing surface is the maintenance item players notice first. Uneven sand, flattened fibers, dirty corners, failed seams and drainage problems can change grip and ball response before the court looks visibly worn.

Cleaning

Remove leaves, paper, dust and organic material without pulling out or concentrating the infill. Corners, walls and the net area deserve extra attention because debris tends to accumulate there.

Brushing and sand redistribution

Brush according to the selected turf system so infill remains evenly distributed. High-traffic zones may need attention sooner than low-use edges. Do not add sand simply because the surface looks different—first confirm the turf manufacturer's target infill quantity and grading.

Specialist maintenance

MONDO recommends periodic specialist work, depending on the surface and condition, to decompact infill, correct distribution and inspect seams and worn areas. Use the Padel Court Turf Guide for the full specification and upkeep checklist.

Glass Maintenance Is More Than Cleaning

Clean glass improves visibility, but inspection is equally important. Check for chipped edges, impact marks, damaged pads or bushings, loose hardware, unintended glass-to-metal contact and unusual panel movement. Any damaged safety-critical panel should be assessed before the court returns to play.

For procurement and inspection details, read the 12 mm tempered-glass and EN 12150 guide.

Steel, Mesh and Corrosion Protection

Outdoor and coastal courts need a documented corrosion inspection routine. Look at welds, cut edges, fasteners, base plates, drainage traps and areas where rackets, cleaning tools or maintenance equipment may damage the finish. Small coating defects are easier to address before corrosion spreads.

High humidity, salt spray and coastal exposure can justify more frequent inspection. A maintenance plan should state the approved touch-up system rather than applying an arbitrary paint product over a galvanized or coated structure.

Drainage Maintenance

A court can have a well-designed drainage system and still become slow to dry if leaves, dirt or compacted infill block the water path. Inspect surface drainage, channels, gullies, roof downpipes and the final outfall. If standing water appears repeatedly, investigate the cause instead of only sweeping the surface.

See Why Does a Padel Court Stay Wet? for the full drainage troubleshooting sequence.

Lighting Maintenance

Lighting maintenance should cover more than failed lamps. Clean lenses, confirm that fittings have not moved, check brackets and cables, and compare actual court visibility with the design intent. If players report glare or a dark zone, inspect aiming and output rather than simply adding more fixtures.

For court-specific planning, use the Padel Court Lighting Guide.

How Much Does Padel Court Maintenance Cost?

There is no reliable universal annual maintenance figure. Labor cost, club standards, court count, indoor versus outdoor exposure, climate, vegetation, turf system and replacement events can change the budget substantially.

A better commercial model separates two budgets:

BudgetExamples
Recurring operating maintenanceCleaning labor, brushing, glass cleaning, drainage clearance, minor sand top-up, inspections, small hardware and touch-up work.
Lifecycle reserveTurf renovation or replacement, glass replacement, lighting drivers or fixtures, corrosion repair, net and door hardware, roof membrane or mechanical components.

Do not hide these two categories in one percentage of construction cost. A four-court outdoor coastal venue and a two-court indoor facility can have very different maintenance patterns even when the courts were purchased for similar prices.

How to Build a Maintenance Budget Before Opening

  1. Request the court, turf, lighting and roof operation manuals before handover.
  2. List every consumable and replaceable component.
  3. Identify which tasks can be done by venue staff and which require specialists.
  4. Price local cleaning and maintenance labor.
  5. Keep a small stock of critical fasteners, net parts and approved touch-up materials.
  6. Confirm glass replacement logistics and lead time.
  7. Reserve separately for turf and lighting lifecycle replacement.
  8. Increase inspection frequency for coastal, high-wind or heavily used courts.

The broader investment model is covered in the Padel Court Cost 2026 guide.

When Should Padel Turf Be Replaced?

Age alone is a poor replacement trigger. Surface life depends on usage, turf construction, outdoor exposure, installation and maintenance. Replacement or renovation should be considered when the surface can no longer provide consistent and safe performance after appropriate maintenance.

  • Detached or heavily worn fibers are visible.
  • Seams or line inserts repeatedly fail.
  • Grip becomes uneven across the court.
  • Ball response differs materially between zones.
  • Infill cannot be kept stable or evenly distributed.
  • Drainage and contamination problems cannot be corrected through normal surface maintenance.

Maintenance Records Protect the Venue

Keep a simple maintenance log with date, court number, task, issue found, photo and corrective action. This helps identify recurring failures, supports warranty discussions and makes it easier to plan component replacement before it interrupts bookings.

The LTA guidance also notes that maintenance requirements provided at project completion should be followed, and failure to maintain a court or covering can affect warranty conditions.

Padel Court Maintenance FAQ

How often should a padel court be maintained?

Maintenance is continuous. Busy clubs need frequent cleaning and inspection, with periodic turf, structure, drainage, lighting and roof checks. Follow the actual product manuals and adjust frequency for usage and weather.

How much does padel court maintenance cost?

There is no universal annual number. Build separate budgets for routine operating maintenance and lifecycle component replacement.

How often should padel turf be brushed?

It depends on the turf system and playing hours. Brush as required to keep infill distribution and surface performance consistent, following the turf manufacturer's instructions.

When should padel turf be replaced?

Use condition and performance rather than age alone. Persistent fiber wear, failed seams, uneven grip or inconsistent bounce are signs that renovation or replacement may be required.

Do glass and steel need maintenance?

Yes. Inspect glass edges and hardware, and check steel, mesh, fasteners and coatings for damage or corrosion, especially on outdoor and coastal courts.
