Padel Court Glass: 12mm Tempered Glass, EN 12150 and Safety

Quick answer: the glass wall is both a playing surface and a safety-critical building component. Buyers should verify more than thickness: glass standard, tempering documentation, edge quality, drilled holes, panel tolerances, fixing details, installation clearances, handling and inspection all matter.

Why Glass Quality Is a Buyer Question, Not Just a Specification Line

Recent padel community discussions about shattered panels show why “12 mm tempered glass” is not enough information for a commercial procurement decision. Failures can involve the panel itself, but they can also involve handling damage, edge contact, installation stress, fixings, structural movement or maintenance.

That is why a responsible comparison should treat the glass and its supporting structure as one system.

Thickness is only one part of the glass specification.

Ask for the applicable glass standard, panel drawings, hole positions, edge finish, fixing method, installation clearances and delivery inspection process.

10 mm vs 12 mm Padel Court Glass

Both 10 mm and 12 mm tempered-glass configurations exist in the market. The correct thickness depends on the approved court design, frame configuration, panel size and applicable requirements. SPORTENVO panoramic and super panoramic systems use 12 mm tempered glass.

What EN 12150 Means

EN 12150 is a European product standard for thermally toughened soda lime silicate safety glass. It addresses characteristics of the heat-treated glass product. If EN 12150 is part of your project or supplier claim, request documentation that can be matched to the delivered panel specification.

SPORTENVO lists EN 12150 documentation within its padel quality and compliance package. Review the quality and certifications page for the current document scope.

Why Tempered Glass Can Still Break

Tempering increases strength and changes the break pattern, but no glass is unbreakable. The most important risks for a padel installation include:

  • Edge chips from transport, lifting or installation.
  • Hard contact between glass and steel or concrete.
  • Incorrect hole alignment or overtightened fasteners.
  • Insufficient clearance for movement and tolerances.
  • Impact concentrated near a damaged edge or fixing point.
  • Structural movement that transfers unintended load into a panel.
  • Rare spontaneous breakage associated with inclusions or manufacturing defects.

Installation Details Buyers Should Check

1. No hard glass-to-metal contact

Glass should be isolated from hard structural contact using the approved pads, gaskets, bushings or fixing system. The exact detail should follow the court drawings.

2. Panel gaps and tolerances

Adjacent panels and surrounding steel require controlled clearances. The target gap must come from the approved design; installers should not improvise by forcing a panel into an opening.

3. Hole alignment

Tempered glass cannot be safely re-drilled or cut after tempering. If holes do not align, the root cause should be corrected rather than forcing the hardware.

4. Torque and hardware

Fasteners should be tightened to the approved installation procedure. Over-compression can create local stress, while loose hardware can allow movement and impact.

Maintenance for Commercial Clubs

Glass walls should be included in the venue's planned inspection routine. Cleaning is not the only task. Managers should also look for chipped edges, damaged gaskets, loose hardware, unusual panel movement and changes after major temperature or weather events.

  • Keep the glass clean enough to inspect.
  • Do not use damaged panels in service.
  • Record chips, impacts or replaced hardware.
  • Recheck fixings after the initial settling period and according to the supplier's maintenance instructions.
  • Escalate unexplained movement or recurring breakage to the court supplier and local engineer.

What to Ask a Padel Court Supplier

  1. What exact glass thickness is used on this court model?
  2. What safety-glass standard applies?
  3. Can you provide the relevant test or compliance documentation?
  4. How are panel edges protected during export packing?
  5. What pads, bushings and fasteners are supplied?
  6. What installation gap and torque procedure is required?
  7. Are spare glass panels included or available with the shipment?
  8. What is the replacement and warranty procedure if a panel arrives damaged?

Padel Court Glass FAQ

What glass is used for a padel court?

Commercial courts use tempered safety glass as part of the playing enclosure. SPORTENVO panoramic and super panoramic systems use 12 mm tempered glass.

Is 12 mm tempered glass required for every padel court?

No universal thickness should be assumed for every design. 10 mm and 12 mm configurations exist. Verify the approved court model, standard and local requirements.

What is EN 12150?

EN 12150 is a European product standard for thermally toughened soda lime silicate safety glass. Where specified, the delivered glass should be traceable to the relevant project documentation.

Why can tempered padel glass break?

Possible causes include edge damage, incorrect contact, installation stress, hole or fixing problems, structural movement and certain glass defects. A failure should be investigated rather than assumed to be normal.
