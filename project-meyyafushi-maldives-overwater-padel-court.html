Maldives · Overwater / Marine Environment

Overwater Classic padel court project in Meyyafushi

A Classic padel court installed on an offshore platform in the Maldives, where sea transport, platform access, wind exposure, humidity and salt spray shaped the project.

Company Project Reference · Project facts cross-checked against the existing archive

Project Snapshot

A padel court planned for a marine platform.

This is a highly site-specific reference: the structure and installation process had to respond to salt air, high humidity and offshore logistics rather than a conventional ground-level venue.

ProjectMeyyafushi Maldives
Project TypeOverwater padel court on offshore platform
Completion2025
Court TypeClassic Padel Court
EnvironmentMarine / high humidity / high salinity / sea-breeze exposure
Installation ContextOffshore transport and platform installation
Project Challenges

What shaped the project brief.

01

Marine corrosion

High salinity and persistent humidity placed unusually high demands on steel protection and maintenance planning.

02

Offshore logistics

Court components, glass and materials had to be transported and handled on an offshore platform rather than a conventional job site.

03

Platform installation

Wind, restricted working space and platform conditions influenced assembly sequencing and site safety.

Recorded Project Scope

What the archive confirms.

01

Classic court system

The project archive identifies a Classic padel court as the selected system.

02

Enhanced corrosion-protection process

The recorded treatment included hot-dip galvanizing, zinc-rich epoxy primer and two high-performance anti-corrosion spray coats.

Installation execution

Buyer Takeaways

Use this case as a planning reference.

Coastal projects are not standard outdoor projects.

Salt spray and humidity change the corrosion-protection discussion and should be addressed in the written project specification.

Logistics can become an engineering constraint.

Island and offshore sites may require special handling, access planning and installation sequencing before goods are shipped.

Local structural approval still matters.

The court system, platform, anchoring and local engineering responsibilities must be coordinated for the actual site.
