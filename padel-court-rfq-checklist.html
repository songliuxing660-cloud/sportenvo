Quotation Scope
What should a padel court quotation include?
A useful quotation makes the technical specification, project boundary and exclusions visible. It does not rely on one total price and a short product description.


Quotation itemWhat the buyer should confirm


Court modelExact official system name and project configuration.


Steel structureSteel grade, main sections, wall thicknesses and the quoted structural configuration.


Corrosion protectionThe actual surface-treatment / coating system in the quotation.


Tempered glassThickness, processing / standard where applicable, quantity and hardware scope.


Mesh / enclosureMesh specification, panel scope and related hardware.


TurfSpecified turf system and supplied scope.


LightingFixture type, quantity, power / protection specification where quoted, poles / brackets and electrical exclusions.


FoundationConcrete interface, modular base or other foundation scope; identify what remains local.


Roof / weather protectionWhether open air, Electric Tent, fixed roof or retractable roof is included and what structural interfaces remain project-specific.


Technical drawingsWhich drawings and installation information are included.


PackingExport packing, component identification and packing-list process.


FreightAgreed Incoterm / commercial basis, named place or port and included freight leg.


Installation supportRemote guidance, documents, on-site support if separately agreed, and local labor / lifting responsibilities.


WarrantyWritten component-level terms attached to the actual quotation rather than a generic headline number.


ExclusionsLocal civil works, electrical, drainage, permits, local engineering, customs, inland delivery, unloading and any other excluded items.




Buying rule: compare the same scope before comparing price. A lower headline price can reflect a different specification, different delivery boundary or more exclusions.

Hidden Scope Check
Ask what is not included before approving the quotation.
Most commercial disputes begin at the boundary between supplied equipment and local project work. Put that boundary in writing before the order.




Local Works
Civil / drainage / electrical
Clarify concrete, excavation, drainage, local cabling, power distribution, permits and site restoration.


Logistics
Destination and unloading
Clarify customs, duty / tax, port charges, inland transport, unloading equipment, storage and local handling.


Installation
Labor and technical support
Clarify local labor, lifting, tools, remote guidance, technician travel if separately contracted, site safety and acceptance responsibility.

RFQ FAQ
Common buyer questions before requesting quotations.




What should be included in a padel court RFQ?
Include location, venue, court quantity, site dimensions, site condition, court preference, foundation, weather protection, delivery destination, installation plan and available drawings / photos.


What should the quotation include?
Ask for the exact technical specification, project scope, drawings, packing, freight basis, installation support, warranty and explicit exclusions.


How should I compare suppliers?
Use the same RFQ and compare every return line by line before comparing price.


Do I need to know the court model first?
No. Use Not Sure — Recommend a Solution if the site conditions are clearer than the final product choice.


Should foundation and roof be in the RFQ?
Yes if they are part of the project, because the court, foundation and roof interfaces can affect one another.


What exclusions should I check?
Clarify civil works, drainage, electrical, permits, local engineering, customs, inland delivery, unloading, labor, tools and lifting.


How should shipping be compared?
Compare the same trade term and named place or port, then separate freight from destination charges and site handling.


Should I rank suppliers with one score?
A single score can hide important differences. Compare evidence, scope and responsibilities directly instead.
