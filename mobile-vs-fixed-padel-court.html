Court Selection Guide

Mobile vs Fixed Padel Court: Which Fits Your Project?

Quick Answer: Choose a fixed court when the project is a long-term venue and relocation is not a priority. Review a mobile / relocatable court when the site is leased, temporary, event-led or future relocation matters. A mobile court does not remove the need for foundation, drainage, level, anchoring or structural review; it changes how those requirements are solved.

What Does “Mobile Padel Court” Actually Mean?

SPORTENVO uses Mobile Padel Court as the official court-system name for projects where flexibility and possible future relocation are part of the brief. The word “mobile” should not be read as “place the court anywhere with no preparation.”

  • The ground or supporting structure still needs adequate capacity.
  • Levels and drainage still affect performance and installation.
  • The court still needs a defined load path and anchoring strategy.
  • Dismantling, packing and reinstallation should be considered before the first installation.
  • The future receiving site must also be reviewed before relocation.

For selected projects, the Mobile Court can be coordinated with a Padel Court Modular Foundation Base. The court system and foundation system are related, but they are not the same product.

What Does “Fixed Padel Court” Mean?

A fixed commercial court is planned as a long-term installation at a defined site. SPORTENVO fixed-court directions include Panoramic, Super Panoramic and Classic, with the exact system selected according to venue positioning, visibility, climate, structural requirements and budget.

“Fixed” does not mean every project uses the same foundation or site detail. Indoor slabs, outdoor civil foundations, rooftops, covered venues and high-wind locations can all require different interfaces.

Foundation: The Most Important Misunderstanding

The wrong comparison is “mobile court = no foundation” versus “fixed court = concrete.” The better comparison is:

  • Mobile project: can the site use an engineered base that supports the selected court while preserving flexibility?
  • Fixed project: what permanent foundation / slab / structural interface is appropriate for the actual venue?

Use the Concrete vs Modular Padel Foundation Guide to compare the foundation decision separately from the court model.

Site Preparation, Drainage and Level Still Matter

Both mobile and fixed projects fail when the site is treated as an afterthought. Before shipment or installation, review:

  • usable site dimensions and circulation;
  • ground or structural-deck condition;
  • level survey and finished elevation;
  • surface and perimeter drainage;
  • unloading and lifting access;
  • power and lighting interfaces;
  • local wind and climate exposure;
  • local permits, engineering and civil responsibilities.

The Padel Court Dimensions & Site Planning Guide provides the broader venue-planning framework.

Relocation: Plan It Before You Buy

A court that can theoretically be dismantled is not automatically a well-planned relocatable project. Buyers should confirm how relocation will work in practice:

Relocation questionWhy it matters
Dismantling sequenceGlass, mesh, steel, turf, lighting and base components need a controlled removal plan.
Component conditionReusable parts must be inspected before transport and reinstallation.
PackingComponents need protection for the second transport cycle.
Receiving siteThe new site still needs levels, drainage, capacity, access and anchoring review.
DocumentationAssembly drawings and component identification reduce avoidable reinstallation problems.

When Is a Fixed Court the Better Choice?

A fixed court is usually the clearer starting point when:

  • the site is owned or secured for long-term operation;
  • the venue is a permanent commercial club or sports facility;
  • the project is integrating permanent civil works, buildings or a structural roof;
  • relocation is not part of the business model;
  • the buyer wants the court specification optimized around one known site.

For standard commercial clubs, start with Panoramic Padel Court. For premium showcase venues, compare Super Panoramic. For practical operation and controlled budget, review Classic.

When Is a Mobile Court the Better Starting Point?

Review Mobile first when:

  • the land or property is leased;
  • the project has a defined temporary operating period;
  • future relocation is commercially important;
  • permanent concrete work is constrained or undesirable;
  • the court is part of an event, pop-up or flexible commercial concept.

See the Harwich, UK portable court reference for a documented flexible-site example and the scope boundaries attached to that case.

How Roof and High-Wind Requirements Change the Decision

Do not select mobile or fixed in isolation when the project also needs a roof or is in a high-wind location. A Padel Court with Roof introduces additional structural reactions, drainage and wind exposure. A coastal or exposed project may require review of the Anti-Hurricane Padel Court — FORCE-HX Series and its foundation / anchoring strategy.

In both cases, the court, foundation and weather-protection system should be coordinated as one project.

Compare the Full Procurement Scope

Do not compare quotations only by the phrase “portable” or “fixed.” Normalize the scope line by line:

  • court structure and corrosion protection;
  • 12 mm tempered glass where specified;
  • welded mesh and hardware;
  • turf and lighting;
  • foundation / base system;
  • anchors and interfaces;
  • technical drawings and installation guidance;
  • packing and transport scope;
  • local civil works and lifting;
  • warranty and after-sales scope.

Mobile vs Fixed Padel Court FAQ

What is the main difference between mobile and fixed?

A fixed court is planned as a long-term site installation. A mobile court is planned around flexible installation and possible future relocation. Both still require site engineering and preparation.

Does mobile mean no foundation?

No. A mobile project can use an engineered modular base where suitable, but ground capacity, level, drainage and anchoring still need review.

Which is better for leased land?

Mobile is often the first system to review when future relocation and reduced permanent civil work matter, subject to site and local requirements.

Which is better for a permanent club?

A fixed Panoramic, Super Panoramic or Classic court is usually the clearer starting point when relocation is not part of the business model.

Can a mobile court move later?

Yes when relocation is planned, but dismantling, packing, transport, component condition and the receiving site still need to be managed.

Can a mobile court have a roof?

Potentially, but roof reactions, wind, drainage and foundation requirements make the combined project more complex and project-specific.

Is mobile always cheaper?

No. Compare the complete project scope, including base, site preparation, transport, installation, drainage and weather protection.

What should I send for a recommendation?

Send project location, venue type, court count, site dimensions, ground condition, lease / ownership context, weather exposure and whether future relocation is expected.
